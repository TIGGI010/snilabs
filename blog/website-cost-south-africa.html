PricingHow much does a website cost in South Africa?

By Tiago de Araujo · Updated 3 November 2026 · 9 min read

Short answer: a small business website in South Africa usually costs between R2,000 and R15,000 to build, and from about R100 a month to keep running. Online stores (from about R7,500) and booking systems (from about R18,000) cost more, because "a website" can mean one page or a small piece of software.

This guide covers our own prices, what you get at each level, what pushes the price up, the running costs people forget to ask about, and three worked examples that add up a full first year.

The jump from one page to a business website matters more than it looks. A single page can only rank on Google for one thing. A page per service gives you a chance to show up for "geyser installation Alberton" and "blocked drain Alberton" separately, which is where most local enquiries come from.

What affects the price of a website

Number of pages

Each page has to be planned, written, designed and checked on phones. A three-page site and a twenty-page site are different jobs, even if they look alike on the home page.

Features

Online bookings, card payments, quote forms with file uploads, property or vehicle listings and a second language each add work. A good quote lists them one by one, so you can drop what you don't need yet and add it later.

Who writes the words

Copywriting is the item most often left out of cheap quotes. If you supply the text and photos, the price stays down. If the studio writes it, expect to pay for that time. Either way, the site can't launch until the words exist, so this is also the most common cause of delays.

Custom design or a template

A proven layout adjusted to your brand is quicker than a design drawn from scratch. For most small businesses a well-built template is enough. Custom design starts to pay off when the site itself is part of what you sell, for example a product maker showing off its range.

Branding

If you don't have a logo yet, it has to come from somewhere. Our logo starter is R1,800, a full brand identity is R4,500, and a brand launch package is R9,500. Some businesses add a logo to the website quote; others already have one they are happy with.

Platform

A custom site, a WordPress site and a Wix or Shopify site each carry different build and running costs. We compare them in Wix, WordPress or a custom website?

What an online store costs, and what pushes the price up

An online store starts from R7,500 with us, which covers 20 products loaded, a cart and checkout, Yoco or PayFast card payments, delivery or collection options and email alerts when an order comes in. Store quotes rise faster than ordinary websites because every product and rule needs setting up and testing. These are the main drivers.

Product count

Each product needs a name, a price, photos, a description and a category. Twenty products is an afternoon of careful work. Three hundred is a project in itself. You can keep the price down by loading the rest yourself once you know how, or by sending a clean spreadsheet with every product, price and photo name in it.

Variants

A T-shirt in four sizes and five colours is one product with twenty variants. Each variant may need its own stock count, price and photo. Clothing, shoes and spares often have more variants than products, and that is where the setup time goes.

Delivery rules

A flat delivery fee plus free collection is simple. Rates by province, by weight, free delivery over a certain spend, or live courier quotes each take more work to set up and test. Decide what you actually need for the first few months before you ask for a quote.

Payment gateway fees

Yoco and PayFast charge a fee on each transaction. That fee comes out of your sales, not the build, so it is a running cost that grows as you sell more. Check each provider's current rates before you choose, and build the fee into your prices.

Stock and accounts

If your stock must match a till system or your accounting software, that link adds cost. Many small shops start with stock counted on the website only and connect the rest later. More on this on our online store development page.

Running costs to budget for

These are separate from the build and should be paid in your name, so you own the accounts:

  • Domain: a .co.za costs from about R100 a year. A .com costs more.
  • Hosting: free to a few hundred rand a month, depending on the platform. Most of the sites we build run on Cloudflare's free tier.
  • Business email: charged per person, per month, by Google Workspace or Microsoft 365.
  • Card payments: a fee per transaction to Yoco, PayFast or your bank.
  • AI assistants and chatbots: R450 a month with us, which covers hosting and the AI at normal small-business volumes.
  • Maintenance: every website we build includes Standard care at R550 a month. We explain what you get for that in how much website maintenance costs.

Hidden costs and red flags

  • The domain is registered in the developer's name. If you fall out, you may lose your web address and your email with it. Ask for the domain and every account in your name from the start.
  • A "free" website on a long contract. The build is free, but the monthly fee runs for two or three years and you may not own the site at the end.
  • No fixed price in writing. A quote that says "from" with no list of what is included tends to grow.
  • Copywriting quietly left out. The quote looks cheap until you are asked to write eight pages yourself.
  • Paid plugins and themes. Some WordPress sites rely on licences that renew every year. Ask who pays for them.
  • Full payment up front. Ours is 50% deposit and 50% on approving the preview for projects up to R20,000, and 40/30/30 above that. Paying everything before you have seen anything leaves you with little leverage.
  • No preview before launch. You should see the whole site on a private link and approve it before it goes live.

How to save money on a website

  • Write the words yourself, even roughly. Editing your draft is quicker than writing from nothing.
  • Send good photos. Phone photos in daylight of your real work beat stock images and cost nothing.
  • Start smaller. A business website now and a store later is often cheaper overall than a store nobody uses yet.
  • Keep the first version of a store simple: fewer products, flat delivery, no stock link.
  • Use free hosting where it fits. Simple sites rarely need paid hosting.
  • Choose a care plan that matches how often you change things, or pay by the hour if you rarely do.

Questions to ask before you pay a deposit

  • Is the price fixed in writing, and what exactly does it include?
  • Will the domain and every account be registered in my name?
  • Will I see the site on a private link before it goes live?
  • Who writes the text, and how many rounds of changes are included?
  • How long will it take once I have sent the words and photos?
  • What are the monthly and yearly costs after launch, and who bills them?
  • What happens, and what does it cost, if something breaks after launch?

Common questions

How much does a website cost in South Africa per month?

Once it is built, a simple small business website can cost very little each month. A .co.za domain is about R100 a year, hosting on Cloudflare is free for most of the sites we build, and business email is charged per user per month by Google or Microsoft. Our optional care plans start at R250 a month if you want someone to watch and update the site.

How much does a one page website cost in South Africa?

Our landing page starts at R1,950. That covers one page with up to five sections, a WhatsApp button and a contact form, and it is usually live in about a week. It suits a launch, an event, a campaign, a CV or a sole trader who needs something professional to send people to.

Do I have to pay the full amount upfront for a website?

No. For projects up to R20,000 we take a 50% deposit and the other 50% when you approve the private preview, before the site goes live. Projects over R20,000 are paid in three stages of 40%, 30% and 30%. Be wary of anyone who wants the full amount before any work starts.

How long does it take to build a website for a small business?

Most of our business websites go live 7 to 10 working days after we receive your words and photos. A landing page usually takes about a week. Corporate and 3D sites take four to eight weeks, because there are more pages, more people to sign off and more to test.

Who owns the domain if a web designer registers it?

Whoever's name is on the registration controls it, so make sure it is yours. At Startline Labs the client owns the domain and every account from the start, and third-party costs like the domain and email are billed to you directly by each provider. If a designer insists on registering it in their own name, treat that as a warning sign.
