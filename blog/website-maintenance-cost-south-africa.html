PricingHow much does website maintenance cost in South Africa?

By Tiago de Araujo · Updated 3 November 2026 · 9 min read

Website maintenance in South Africa usually costs from about R250 to R2,000 a month for a small business, depending on how many changes are included and how quickly they are done. Some businesses skip the monthly fee and pay R450 an hour when they need something.

Here is what you are paying for, what each option costs over a year, and when a plan is worth it.

What website maintenance should cover

"Maintenance" is a loose word, and quotes for it vary because people mean different things by it. A useful plan covers four jobs:

  • Watching: checking the site is online, and that the SSL certificate and domain renew on time. A lapsed domain takes your email down with it.
  • Backups and updates: keeping copies of the site and applying security updates, as far as the platform allows.
  • Fixes: repairing anything that breaks, such as a contact form that stops sending.
  • Small changes: new prices, photos, team members, specials or opening hours.

Some plans also include marketing work, such as Google Business Profile posts and a monthly report. That is useful, but it is a different service from keeping the site healthy, so check which one you are being quoted for.

What affects the cost of maintenance

The platform

A simple custom website with no plugins needs very little looking after. There is less to update and less that can break. A WordPress site with plugins needs regular updates, because outdated plugins are one of the most common ways small business sites get hacked. We compare the platforms in Wix, WordPress or a custom website?

How often you change things

A plumber might change prices twice a year. A restaurant changes its specials every week. The more often you need changes, the more a plan with included time makes sense.

How fast you need changes done

Our Standard plan does changes within 3 working days. If you need same-day work on a store during a sale, that is a Managed plan with agreed response times, and it costs more.

What the site does

A brochure site that only shows information is cheap to look after. An online store handles orders and payments, and a booking system handles appointments. When those break, you lose money by the hour, so they need closer watching.

AI assistants and chatbots

If your site has an AI assistant, that runs at R450 a month with us, which covers hosting and the AI at normal small-business volumes. On WhatsApp, Meta also charges a small fee per message, billed to you. Since 1 October 2026, service replies are free for the first 1,000 a month per number; other message types are charged. Signs your website needs attention now

Whether or not you pay for a plan, these are signs that something has been neglected and should be looked at soon:

  • A "not secure" warning in the browser. The SSL certificate has lapsed, and many visitors will leave before the page loads.
  • Enquiries have gone quiet. Send yourself a test message through the contact form. Forms that stop sending are common and easy to miss.
  • Old prices, staff or opening hours. Customers notice, and Google may show the wrong details if your profile and site disagree.
  • The site is slow on a phone. Large photos and outdated plugins are the usual cause.
  • You don't know who has the logins. If the person who built the site has moved on, recover the domain and hosting accounts before you need them.

Most of these take under an hour to fix once someone is looking. The cost of leaving them is lost enquiries, which you never see.

Hidden costs and red flags

  • A plan with no list of what is included. "Maintenance, R800 a month" tells you nothing. Ask for the tasks in writing.
  • Changes that are "included" with no time limit, which usually means they get done when the developer has a gap.
  • Domain or hosting held in the developer's name. If they disappear, so does your access. You should own every account.
  • Long lock-in contracts. A care plan should be month to month or have a clear notice period.
  • Plugin licence renewals that arrive as a surprise invoice once a year.
  • No backups you can reach. Ask where they are stored and how quickly a site can be restored.

How to save money on maintenance

  • Choose a simpler platform at build time. Fewer plugins means less to update and less to go wrong.
  • Batch your changes. Send a list once a quarter rather than one item a week, and pay by the hour.
  • Learn the easy edits yourself, such as opening hours or a special, and leave fixes and backups to someone else.
  • Match the plan to your season. Move up to Growth before a busy period and back down after it.
  • Keep accounts in your name so you can move to another provider without paying to recover them.

Questions to ask before you sign a maintenance plan

  • What exactly is included each month, in writing?
  • How many minutes or hours of changes, and what happens to unused time?
  • How fast are changes done, and how fast are breakages fixed?
  • Where are the backups kept, and have you tested restoring one?
  • Are the domain, hosting and email accounts in my name?
  • What is the notice period if I want to cancel?
  • Which costs are billed by other providers, and roughly how much are they?

Do you need a maintenance plan?

A simple custom website with no plugins needs very little looking after, and paying by the hour is often fine. A WordPress site with plugins, an online store or a booking system needs regular attention to stay secure and working, so a plan is worth it there. Website maintenance

Common questions

Do I need to pay monthly for my website in South Africa?

Not always. You will pay for the domain, about R100 a year for a .co.za, and for business email per user per month. Hosting is free for most sites we build on Cloudflare. A maintenance plan is optional for a simple custom site, but worth it for WordPress sites and online stores.

What is included in website maintenance?

A basic plan should cover uptime checks, watching that your SSL certificate and domain renew, backups and the updates your platform supports, and fixes for anything that breaks. Our Basic plan does that for R250 a month. Higher plans add time for small changes, such as new prices, photos or opening hours, and monthly reporting.

How much does it cost to update my website?

One-off updates to an existing website start at R1,500 with us, and hourly work is R450 an hour. If you change things often, a care plan is usually cheaper: Standard at R550 a month includes up to 30 minutes of changes, done within three working days, and Growth at R1,200 includes up to two hours.

What happens if I do not maintain my WordPress website?

Plugins and the WordPress core fall out of date, and a neglected WordPress site is one of the most common ways small businesses get hacked. An expired SSL certificate or domain can also take the site offline without warning. Regular updates, backups and someone watching the renewals prevent most of these problems.

Can my web designer post on my Google Business Profile for me?

Yes, if it is part of your plan. Our Growth care plan, at R1,200 a month, includes two Google Business Profile posts a month, up to two hours of website changes and a monthly report, on top of the Basic checks, backups and fixes. It suits businesses that rely on local search for new customers.
