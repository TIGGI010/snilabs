AI and automationWhatsApp chatbots for small business: cost and what they do

By Tiago de Araujo · Updated 3 November 2026 · 9 min read

A WhatsApp AI chatbot for a South African small business costs from about R4,500 to set up and R450 a month to run, plus Meta's own per-message fees. For a typical small business, year one comes to roughly R10,000 to R15,000 all in, depending mostly on how many reminders and promotions you send.

Below is what a chatbot does, what each part costs, three worked examples, how it compares with Meta's own Business AI and with hiring someone, and what to ask before you pay.

What a WhatsApp chatbot can do

  • Answer questions about prices, hours, location and services from your own information.
  • Take bookings and send confirmations and reminders.
  • Collect the details for a quote, including photos and measurements.
  • Check an order or booking status in your own system.
  • Hand the chat to a real person when the customer asks, or when the question is outside what it knows.
  • Reply at 2am, on public holidays and while you are busy with a customer.

A good chatbot should not guess. If it does not know, it says so and passes the chat on. It should not promise discounts or delivery dates you have not given it, and customers should be able to tell they are talking to an assistant.

What affects the price

How much it needs to know

A chatbot that answers twenty common questions from one page of information is quick to set up. One that covers three branches, a full price list and different rules for weekends takes longer to load and test.

Whether it connects to your systems

Answering questions needs only your information. Taking a real booking, checking an order or creating a quote means reading and writing to another system, which is where most of the extra work sits.

How many messages it handles

The R450 a month covers normal small-business volumes. A business handling thousands of chats a month uses more AI and hosting, so we price that on use and agree it upfront.

What you send first

Meta's fees depend mostly on messages you start yourself. Replying to customers is free up to 1,000 a month per number. Reminders and promotions are charged per message.

Three worked examples for year one

Our setup and monthly fees are fixed. Meta's fees are approximate, using the upper end of the ranges above.

1. Salon booking chatbot

A salon gets about 600 WhatsApp chats a month, books clients in and sends a reminder the day before each of about 300 appointments. Once a month it sends a special to 200 clients who opted in.

  • Setup: R4,500
  • Running: R450 × 12 = R5,400
  • Service replies: about 600 a month, under the free 1,000, so R0
  • Reminders: 300 × about 20c = about R60 a month, about R720 for the year
  • Promotions: 200 × about 70c = about R140 a month, about R1,680 for the year
  • Year one: about R12,300

2. Quote intake for a fabrication workshop

A steel fabrication workshop uses the chatbot to collect drawings, photos and measurements for gates, burglar bars and carports, then sends the quote request to the office. About 80 times a month it sends a "your quote is ready" message. It sends no promotions.

  • Setup: R4,500
  • Running: R450 × 12 = R5,400
  • Service replies: under 1,000 a month, so R0
  • Quote-ready messages: 80 × about 20c = about R16 a month, about R192 for the year
  • Year one: about R10,092

3. Gym or personal trainer

A small gym answers about 800 chats a month, sends about 1,200 class reminders a month, and sends two promotions a month to 400 opted-in members.

  • Setup: R4,500
  • Running: R450 × 12 = R5,400
  • Service replies: under 1,000 a month, so R0
  • Reminders: 1,200 × about 15c = about R180 a month, about R2,160 for the year
  • Promotions: 800 × about 70c = about R560 a month, about R6,720 for the year
  • Year one: about R18,780

In the gym example, promotions are the biggest cost after our fees. Sending one well-aimed campaign a month instead of two would save about R3,360 a year. Gyms that want bookings, payments and check-ins in one place often pair the chatbot with an app; see apps for fitness coaches.

Meta's Business AI or a custom chatbot?

Meta now offers its own Business AI agent for WhatsApp Business. It launched globally in June 2026 as part of the WhatsApp Business Premium tiers, and it answers customers using the information you give it about your business. Check Meta's current pricing and what your tier includes.

For many small businesses that is enough. If you mainly need common questions answered after hours, try Meta's option first. It may cost less, and you can always move to a custom chatbot later.

A custom chatbot earns its cost when it needs to do work in your own systems:

  • It books into your real calendar, so there are no double bookings.
  • It checks an order, a job or stock levels and gives a live answer.
  • It works out a quote from your own pricing rules and sends it to the office.
  • It hands over to staff with the full chat and the customer's details already saved where your team works.
  • The same assistant answers on your website as well as WhatsApp.
  • You want control over where chat data is stored and how long it is kept, for POPIA.

If none of those apply, start with Meta's built-in option.

These are not either-or. The best setups we see use a chatbot for the routine questions and a person for the chats that need one.

Hidden costs to ask about

  • Your time upfront. Someone has to write down prices, hours, policies and the answers to common questions. A chatbot is only as good as that information.
  • Keeping it current. When prices or hours change, the chatbot must be updated. Ask whether that is included or charged by the hour. Our rate is R450 an hour.
  • Meta's setup steps. Your business usually needs to be verified with Meta, your display name approved, and every reminder or promotion template approved before it can be sent.
  • Your existing number. Ask how your current WhatsApp number will be moved or shared, and what happens to its chat history.
  • Marketing volume. At roughly 70c each, promotions to a large list add up quickly.
  • Busy months. Find out what counts as normal volume and what happens to the price if you go over it.

How to keep the cost down

  • Start with answers and handover only. Add bookings and system connections once you see what customers ask most.
  • Let customers message you first, through a WhatsApp button on your website and Google profile. Replies are free up to 1,000 a month.
  • Send reminders as utility messages, not promotions, and keep promotions to people who opted in.
  • Keep your prices, hours and policies in one document, so updates are quick.

Questions to ask before you pay a deposit

  • Will the Meta Business account and WhatsApp number be in my name?
  • What exactly does the monthly fee cover, and at what volume does it change?
  • What does the chatbot say when it does not know the answer?
  • How does handover to a person work, and who gets notified?
  • Where are the chats stored, for how long, and who can read them? Our POPIA checklist covers what to look for.
  • Can I read the transcripts and see what customers ask most?
  • If I stop the service, what happens to my number and my data?

Common questions

How much does a WhatsApp chatbot cost in South Africa?

Our AI chatbots for WhatsApp or your website start from R4,500 to set up, plus R450 a month to run. The monthly fee covers hosting and the AI at normal small-business volumes. Meta also charges its own small per-message fees on WhatsApp, billed directly to you, so check Meta's current rate card for the message types you plan to send.

Does the WhatsApp Business API charge per message in South Africa?

Yes, for some messages. Since 1 October 2026, service replies to customers who message you are free for the first 1,000 a month per number, then charged. Utility messages you start, such as reminders, cost roughly 15 to 20 cents each, and marketing messages roughly 70 cents. Rates change, so check Meta's rate card before planning a campaign.

Can a WhatsApp chatbot take bookings?

Yes. A chatbot can take bookings, send reminders and collect the details for a quote, including photos, then hand the chat to a person when needed. If you also want staff calendars, payments or a customer login, that becomes a booking system, which we build as an app from R18,000.

Will customers know they are talking to a bot?

They should. A good chatbot tells customers they are speaking to an assistant, answers only from the information you give it, and hands the chat to a real person when it does not know or when the customer asks. It should never guess prices, promise discounts or commit to delivery dates you have not approved.

Can I use my personal WhatsApp number for a chatbot?

It is better not to. A chatbot runs on a WhatsApp Business number, ideally one that is not already in use on a personal phone. Before setting one up you will also need your prices, hours, common questions and policies written down, and a decision on who takes over when the bot passes a chat on.
