AI and automationHow to set up AI for your small business

By Tiago de Araujo · Updated 3 November 2026 · 5 min read

Setting up AI for a small business mostly means giving ChatGPT or Claude the right instructions once, so your team can draft quotes, emails, posts and admin in minutes instead of starting from a blank page. You don't need new software or a developer to begin. You need a business account, a handful of good templates, a few house rules and about half a day.

Here is what AI is useful for, what to keep away from it, and a simple way to set it up.

What AI is good at in a small business

AI tools are best at first drafts of work you already know how to judge. You stay the expert and it does the typing.

  • Quotes and proposals. Paste your rough job notes and get a quote laid out in your format, with scope, exclusions and payment terms. You check every figure before it goes out.
  • Emails and replies. Follow-ups on quotes, polite payment reminders, replies to complaints and supplier queries, in your tone rather than a robotic one.
  • Social media posts. Captions from a photo of a finished job, a month of post ideas, or one message rewritten for Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn.
  • Admin. Meeting notes turned into action lists, a voice note turned into a step-by-step procedure, spreadsheet formulas explained, long supplier terms summarised.
  • Customer FAQs. Write a clear set of answers once, then reuse them on your website, in WhatsApp quick replies, or as the knowledge behind a chatbot.

What not to hand over to AI

AI writes confidently even when it is wrong. That is fine for a draft caption and risky for anything with legal or money consequences.

  • Legal and financial decisions. It can explain a clause or a tax term in plain language, but contracts, tax returns, credit decisions and disputes still belong with your accountant or attorney.
  • Numbers you haven't checked. It can add up wrongly, use an old price or invent a figure that looks right. Every total on a quote gets checked by a person.
  • Customer personal information. Under the Protection of Personal Information Act you stay responsible for personal information you pass to another service. Don't paste ID numbers, bank details, medical details or full customer lists into a chat tool. Business plans usually give you more control over how data is stored and used, so read the settings and terms of the plan you choose.
  • Anything sent unread. A person reads every message before it reaches a customer. That rule alone prevents most AI mistakes.

How to set up AI in five steps

1. Pick one tool and a business account

ChatGPT and Claude both work well for this. Choose one, put it on a business email address rather than someone's personal account, and pay for the plan per user, per month, so the work stays with the business if someone leaves.

2. List the jobs you repeat

Write down five to ten tasks that eat time every week. Quotes, follow-ups and social posts are on almost every list. Start with the two that annoy you most.

3. Build a template for each job

A template tells the AI who you are, who the reader is, what tone to use, what format to follow and what to ask you for if details are missing. For example: "You write quotes for a steel fabrication business in Alberton. Use our standard layout, list exclusions, quote in rand including VAT, and ask me for site access details if I haven't given them." Save these in a project or custom instructions so nobody has to retype them.

4. Agree house rules

Put them on one page: which tool the business uses, what information never goes in, who checks prices, and that nothing is sent without a read-through. Keep it short enough that people actually follow it.

5. Train the team, then review after a month

Show everyone the templates on their own jobs, not on made-up examples. After a month, ask what worked, fix the templates that give poor drafts and drop the ones nobody uses.

How much time can it save?

It depends on how repetitive your work is, so treat these as examples rather than promises:

  • A quote that used to take 40 minutes to type might take 10 minutes to check and adjust.
  • A week of social media captions can be drafted in one sitting of about 20 minutes.
  • A firm but friendly payment reminder goes from a 15-minute struggle to a two-minute edit.
  • Notes from a site meeting become a clean action list before you leave the parking lot.

The saving comes from not starting from nothing. The checking time doesn't go away, and it shouldn't.

Common questions

What can a small business use AI for?

Most small businesses get the quickest wins from first drafts: quotes, follow-up emails, payment reminders, social media captions, meeting notes and answers to common customer questions. A person still checks every figure and reads every message before it goes out. AI saves time on the typing, not on the judgement, so it works best on tasks you already know how to do well.

Is ChatGPT or Claude better for a small business?

Both handle quotes, emails, posts and admin well, and the difference for everyday small business work is smaller than people expect. Pick the one your team finds easier, put it on a business account rather than a personal one, and save your templates in it. Consistency matters more than the brand, because the templates are where most of the value sits.

Is it safe to put customer information into AI tools under POPIA?

Be careful. Under POPIA you remain responsible for personal information you share with another service. Avoid pasting ID numbers, bank details, medical details or customer lists into a chat tool, and read the data settings and terms of the plan you use. Business plans usually give more control. This is general information, not legal advice, so ask an attorney about complex cases.
