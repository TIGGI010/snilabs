AppsHow much does an app cost in South Africa?

By Tiago de Araujo · Updated 3 November 2026 · 9 min read

A business app in South Africa usually costs between R18,000 and R110,000 to build. A simple web app for staff starts at about R18,000, while an app in both app stores with logins, payments and an admin dashboard is usually R60,000 or more, plus a few hundred rand a month to run.

Below is how that price comes together: where the app runs, which features cost the most, what you get at different budgets, three worked examples for year one, and the running costs people forget to ask about.

What an app costs by where it runs

The first decision is where people will use the app. These are the starting prices in our app planner, before features are added.

A web app is often the cheapest way to start. Staff and business clients will happily open a link, and the same app works on phones, tablets and computers. Android makes sense when you serve the South African public, because most phones here run Android. iPhone usually comes once the app has proven itself. We cover that choice in more detail in web, Android or iPhone: which to build first.

What affects the price

Features

Features move the price more than anything else. Each one has to be designed, built and tested on real phones. These are the typical additions in our planner:

  • Accounts and login: about R4,000
  • Card payments: about R6,000
  • Bookings calendar: about R5,000
  • Push notifications: about R3,000
  • QR codes: about R2,500
  • Maps and location: about R4,500
  • Admin dashboard: about R6,500
  • Chat between users: about R7,000
  • An AI assistant: about R8,000

Number of user types

An app used only by your staff is one job. An app with customers, staff and an admin is three apps sharing a database, each with its own screens and permissions. Every extra role adds design and testing time.

Screens and design

A clean, proven design is included. The first 12 screens are included, and each extra screen adds about R900. Custom-designed screens add about R6,000, and custom design with a logo and brand about R9,500.

Connections to other software

Pulling stock from your accounting system, sending invoices or linking to an existing booking tool depends on how well that software lets others connect. Good documentation keeps it quick. No documentation, or no access, can double the time for that part.

How clear the brief is

A written list of who uses the app and what version one does is the cheapest thing you can bring to a quote. Vague briefs lead to wide price ranges, because the studio has to price in the unknowns.

What you get at about R18,000 vs R35,000 vs R60,000+

Only the R18,000 figure is one of our published starting prices. The other two columns are rough guides to the kind of scope that usually sits at those budgets. Every app is quoted per project once the scope is written down.

The bands overlap. A web app with a lot of features can cost more than a simple Android app, which is why a feature list matters more than the platform alone.

Three worked examples for year one

These use the planner prices above. Hosting and exchange rates are approximate, and card fees are left out because they depend on your sales.

1. Job-card web app for a fabrication workshop

The workshop wants technicians to open a job on a phone, log hours and materials, add photos and mark it done, with the office seeing everything on a dashboard.

  • Web app: R18,000
  • Accounts and login: R4,000
  • Admin dashboard: R6,500
  • Build total: R28,500, paid 40/30/30 as R11,400, R8,550 and R8,550
  • Hosting, roughly R300 a month: about R3,600 for the year
  • Year one: about R32,100, with no store fees

2. Class booking app for a gym or personal trainer

Members book classes, pay for a class pack, get a reminder and check in with a QR code at the door. The trainer manages it all from an admin screen. More on this kind of build on our apps for fitness coaches page and in what a gym app costs.

  • Android app: R35,000
  • Accounts R4,000, bookings R5,000, card payments R6,000, push notifications R3,000, QR codes R2,500, admin dashboard R6,500: R27,000
  • Build total: R62,000
  • Google Play fee of US$25: roughly R450 at about R18 to the dollar
  • Hosting, roughly R400 a month: about R4,800 for the year
  • Year one: about R67,250

The same features as a web app come to R45,000 to build (R18,000 plus R27,000), or about R49,800 for year one with the same hosting. That is why many gyms start on the web.

3. Ordering app for a delivery business, on both stores

  • Android and iPhone: R50,000
  • Accounts R4,000, card payments R6,000, maps and location R4,500, push notifications R3,000, admin dashboard R6,500: R24,000
  • Build total: R74,000
  • Store fees of US$25 plus US$99: roughly R2,230 at about R18 to the dollar
  • Hosting, roughly R600 a month: about R7,200 for the year
  • Year one: about R83,430

Running costs after launch

The build is a once-off. These continue every month or year and should be paid from accounts in your name:

  • Hosting and database: usually a few hundred rand a month for a small app, growing with users and stored files such as photos.
  • Google Play: a once-off US$25 developer fee.
  • Apple Developer Program: US$99 every year. If it lapses, the app is removed from the App Store.
  • Card payments: a fee per transaction to Yoco, PayFast or your payment provider.
  • Messages: SMS, email sending services and WhatsApp messages are charged by each provider.
  • Support and changes: optional, either on a monthly plan or by the hour. Our hourly rate is R450.

Hidden costs to ask about

  • Yearly platform updates. Apple and Google release new versions of their systems every year, and Google Play requires apps to target recent versions. Older apps need small updates to stay listed.
  • Third-party services. Maps, SMS and AI features often have free tiers that run out as you grow. Ask which services the app uses and what they cost at ten times your current users.
  • Content and data entry. Loading products, members or price lists takes time. Find out whether it is in the quote or left to you.
  • Store review delays. A new personal Google Play account must run a closed test with at least 12 testers for 14 days before going public. Our guide to publishing on Google Play and the App Store explains the steps.
  • Personal information. An app that stores customer details falls under POPIA. A security and POPIA review from us costs R4,500.

How to keep the cost down

  • Start with a clickable prototype. You tap through the screens on your phone before any code is written, and changes cost hours instead of days.
  • Build version one around one job. Write a separate list for version two and leave it alone until version one is in use.
  • Start on the web or Android. Add iPhone once the numbers justify it. The back end is reused, so you do not start again.
  • Use a proven design. Custom screens look good in a pitch, but most users care whether the app is quick and clear.
  • Use ready-made tools where they fit. If you only need people to book a slot, a booking system may do the job for less than a full app. If customers mostly ask questions on WhatsApp, an AI chatbot from R4,500 may come first.
  • Bring your content and decisions on time. Waiting for logins, text and approvals is the most common reason projects run long. See how long an app takes to build.

Questions to ask before you pay a deposit

  • Is the price fixed in writing, with every feature and screen listed?
  • Will the Google Play and Apple accounts, hosting and domain be registered in my name from the start?
  • Do I own the code, and will I get access to it?
  • Can I test the app on my own phone during the build, not only at the end?
  • How are payments split? Ours are 50/50 up to R20,000 and 40/30/30 above that.
  • What happens if Apple or Google rejects the app?
  • What will it cost to run each month at my expected number of users?
  • Who fixes bugs after launch, for how long, and at what rate?

If a studio cannot answer these clearly, the low quote may not stay low. Our app development page shows how we work, from the written plan to launch.

Common questions

What is the cheapest way to build an app for my business?

Start with a web app built around one main job. Our apps and business systems start at R18,000, and a web app can be installed on a phone's home screen without the app stores. Test the idea with a clickable prototype first, then add Android and iPhone versions once people are using it.

What are the monthly costs of running an app?

Hosting for a small app is usually a few hundred rand a month and grows with the number of users. Google Play charges a once-off US$25 developer fee and Apple charges US$99 a year, both paid in your name. If the app includes an AI assistant or sends WhatsApp messages, those carry their own running costs too.

Do I pay for an app upfront or in stages?

In stages. For projects over R20,000, which includes most apps, we split payment into 40%, 30% and 30% across the project. Smaller jobs up to R20,000 are a 50% deposit and 50% when you approve the preview before launch. You own the domain and every account from the start.

Who owns the app when a developer builds it?

You should. Ask for the app to be published under your own Google Play and Apple developer accounts, with the domain and hosting in your name too. At Startline Labs the client owns the domain and every account from the start. If a developer holds the store account, they effectively control your app.

Can I add features to my app later?

Yes. A well-planned version one does the single most important job, and features such as payments, push notifications, chat or an admin dashboard can be added in later stages without starting again. Keep a list for version two rather than adding features halfway through the build, which is one of the main reasons app projects run late.
